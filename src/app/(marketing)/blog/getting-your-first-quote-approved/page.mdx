# What makes a journalist actually use your quote

Most expert-outreach pitches get ignored, and it's rarely because the expert wasn't qualified. It's because the pitch didn't do the one thing a journalist on deadline needs: answer the actual question, fast, with something they can paste in.

## Answer the question that was asked

A journalist asking "what's driving churn in mid-market SaaS" doesn't want your general philosophy on customer success. They want one or two concrete reasons, ideally with a number or an example attached. If your first sentence doesn't answer the question, the rest goes unread.

## Specific beats credentialed

"I've worked in this space for 12 years" tells a journalist nothing they can use. "We saw churn drop 40% after moving onboarding from email to in-app" is a sentence they can build a paragraph around. Lead with the specific thing, and let your title do the credentialing.

## Respect the deadline in the subject line

A quote that arrives after the article ships is worth nothing, no matter how good it is. If you're not going to make the deadline, don't send a rushed version — skip it and wait for the next query that fits.

## Say the thing you'd actually say

Ghostwritten quotes that sound like nobody read them back are easy to spot, and they're the ones that get cut in editing. A journalist is putting your name on this. It should sound like you.
