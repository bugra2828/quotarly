# How to build backlinks without guest posting

Guest posting still works, technically. But it's gotten slower, more expensive, and riskier than it used to be — and there's a cheaper way to get links that are arguably better anyway.

## Why guest posting has gotten worse

A few things changed:

- **The good sites stopped needing you.** High-authority blogs with real traffic don't need free content from strangers; they have their own writers. What's left accepting pitches is mostly lower-authority sites, which pass less value, or paid placements disguised as guest posts.
- **Google has gotten better at spotting link networks.** A backlink from a site whose entire content strategy is "accept guest posts for links" looks exactly like what it is. These links carry less weight than they used to, and in some cases attract scrutiny rather than ranking benefit.
- **The outreach cost didn't go down.** You're still writing a full article, pitching editors who mostly say no, and waiting weeks for publication — for a link that's worth less than it was three years ago.

## The alternative: earned mentions from being quoted

Journalists publish dozens of articles a week that need outside expertise — a stat, a quote, an example. When your answer gets used, you typically get a link back to your site as the source. The mechanics are almost the opposite of guest posting:

- **Time investment:** a full article (hours) vs. a few sentences (minutes).
- **Approval odds:** low with guest posting, since editors reject most pitches, vs. higher with quote mentions, since the journalist needs *an* answer, not *your* answer specifically.
- **Link context:** a site built to host guest content vs. a real news or industry site, inside an actual reported article.
- **Repeatability:** limited by how many sites will take your pitch vs. limited only by how many relevant requests you answer.

The link itself tends to carry more weight too, because it's sitting inside a genuinely reported article on a site with real editorial standards — not a paid-link page dressed up as content.

## What this actually requires

Mainly volume and speed. Journalist source requests go out constantly, and most people don't see them in time or don't bother answering. The expert profiles that build up a real backlink portfolio this way aren't doing anything exotic — they're just consistently answering the handful of requests that genuinely fit their expertise, with a specific, usable answer, before the window closes.

It's slower to start than guest posting (you're waiting for the right request, not writing on your own schedule), but far cheaper per link, and the links are harder to dismiss as manufactured — because they aren't.
