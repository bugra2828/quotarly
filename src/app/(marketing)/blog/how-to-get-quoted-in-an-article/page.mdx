# How to get quoted in an article, from zero

Getting quoted isn't about being famous or having a publicist. It's about seeing a journalist's request before enough other people do, and answering it in a way that's easy to use. Here's the mechanical version, stripped of the mystique.

## Where the requests actually come from

Journalists on deadline don't have time to cold-source every article, so a handful of newsletters exist specifically to collect their requests and forward them to anyone who signs up. A typical one looks like: outlet name, a one-line topic, three or four interview questions, a reply email, a deadline. Dozens of these land every day across different niches — business, health, tech, lifestyle. The volume is the opportunity: most of them get only a handful of responses, because most people never see them or don't bother answering.

## What to actually send

Three things, in this order:

1. **A direct answer to the specific question**, not a restatement of your general expertise. If they asked "what's one mistake founders make when pricing a SaaS product," open with the mistake — not with "Great question, I've worked in SaaS for 10 years."
2. **One concrete detail** — a number, a before/after, a specific example — that makes the answer feel earned rather than generic.
3. **Your name, title, and a one-line bio**, at the end, not the start. Journalists skim for the answer first and the credential second.

## What beginners get wrong

- **Pitching every query instead of the right ones.** A mediocre quote on a loosely-related topic doesn't help you; it trains you to write generic answers, and journalists ignore generic answers. Answer the ten queries that are a genuine fit, not the hundred that are vaguely adjacent.
- **Writing like a press release.** "We're thrilled to share that..." gets cut in the first editing pass. Write the way you'd actually explain it to a colleague.
- **Missing the deadline window.** Most of these requests get answered — and closed — within hours, not days. If you're checking once a week, you're seeing only the leftovers.
- **No link.** If the point is building your profile or your site's authority, make sure your reply includes the URL you actually want credited, not just your name.

## What it looks like once it's working

The pattern, once you've done it a dozen times, is boring in a good way: a request lands, you can tell in ten seconds whether it fits, you send three sentences that directly answer it, and either it gets used or it doesn't. The founders who get quoted regularly aren't more interesting than everyone else answering the same newsletters — they just reply faster, more specifically, and more often.
